In 1948 a young	  engineer joined Bell Labs. The engineer didn't know
what to expect. "Welcome to the lab," said Shannon. "I need a machine
that predicts the next word!"

The engineer asked how the machine should work. Shannon opened a book
and pointed at a word. "Find this word again. Write down the word that
follows it. Then find that word, and repeat."

The engineer wrote the first program that afternoon. The program read the
book and counted every pair of words. It wasn't fast, but it worked!

Shannon looked at the output and laughed. The machine wrote sentences
that almost made sense. The machine wrote "the engineer wrote the machine"
and stopped. ?! Nobody could explain the last line.

The next day the engineer added a second table. The second table counted
single words. With both tables the machine could score a sentence. A real
sentence scored higher than a scrambled sentence. Shannon was pleased.
The engineer was pleased too. It was a good day at the lab.
The engineer left a note
  without a final period
